intro: |
  **Cognitive network science** studies human cognition by modelling the *mental lexicon*, the store of words and concepts behind thought and communication, as a network.

  Traditional psycholinguistics focuses on the properties of individual words, such as valence, arousal or concreteness. Cognitive network science looks at the **structure that emerges from their connections**. Feature-rich network models bring the two views together, relating word properties and connectivity to language functions and cognitive processes.

spec:
  - label: System
    value: Human cognition, mental lexicon, semantic, phonological and syntactic memory.
  - label: Nodes
    value: Concepts and words, or their sub-units such as phonemes and morphemes.
  - label: Relations
    value: Free associations, synonyms and antonyms, hypernymy, shared features, phonological similarity, syntactic dependencies.
  - label: Graph
    value: Words are linked when they share a linguistic similarity, are recalled together, or co-occur in sentences.
  - label: Higher-order
    value: Co-occurrences and multi-response association tasks naturally form hyperedges, revealing structures such as knowledge gaps.
  - label: Attributes
    value: Length, frequency, polysemy, valence, arousal, dominance and concreteness, related to connectivity through assortative mixing.
  - label: Multilayer
    value: Associations, synonyms and phonological similarities as layers, analysed together to reveal patterns in word acquisition.
  - label: Dynamic
    value: The lexicon grows as words are learned; acquisition can be modelled as a process, such as a random walk, on an evolving network.
objectives:
  - Develop a feature-rich network framework for the study of human cognition, the mental lexicon and language acquisition.
  - Pursue data-driven cognitive network science, applying these tools to data from psycholinguistic experiments.
  - Define sound methodologies to extract emotional profiles from user-generated content.
