intro: |
  The Web and online social networks have turned everyone into a potential news medium. Direct, unfiltered communication makes access to information more democratic, but it also **exposes readers to manipulative, biased and disinformative content**.

  The *pollution* of public debate is a significant issue for modern democracies. With no recognised authority vouching for quality, and with recommender systems that reinforce confirmation bias, it becomes hard to judge the information people actually consume. We study these **Polluted Information Environments** (PIEs) with data-driven network models.

objectives:
  - Build a data-driven framework for online debates, combining AI/ML data enrichment with higher-order, feature-rich and dynamic network models.
  - Apply it to Polluted Information Environments to study how they form and evolve, and how predictable their evolution is.
  - Measure pollution with statistically sound inference, evaluate mitigation strategies and estimate how predictable pollution dynamics are.
