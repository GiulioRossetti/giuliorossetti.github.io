Research line

Network Modeling

Dynamic, multilayer, higher-order and feature-rich network models, with algorithms and statistical validation for networked data.

OverviewBeyond simple graphsObjectivesPublications

Networks describe countless real-world phenomena, from biological interactions to human behaviour, as entities connected by relations: users exchanging messages on a platform, for instance, become nodes linked by edges.

Classic network science often treats social interactions as simple, static, pairwise graphs. That abstraction is useful, but too coarse for phenomena that are dynamic, multi-resolution and not necessarily binary. We develop enriched network models that capture this complexity, together with the algorithms and validation methods needed to use them.

Beyond simple graphs

Four complementary ways of enriching the network abstraction, often studied in isolation and increasingly combined in our work.

Dynamic networks

Nodes and edges appear and vanish over time; models keep track of when interactions happen, not only whether they happen.

Multilayer networks

The same actors connected by different kinds of relations, such as friendship, communication and collaboration, analysed jointly.

Higher-order networks

Hypergraphs and simplicial complexes capture group interactions that cannot be reduced to pairs.

Feature-rich networks

Node and edge attributes, such as opinions, demographics or text, enter the model alongside the topology.

Objectives

  1. 1

    Define enhanced network models that describe complex phenomena, fusing feature-rich, dynamic and higher-order representations.

  2. 2

    Design novel algorithms to extract valuable knowledge from networked data.

  3. 3

    Develop sound methodologies to statistically validate network models and algorithms.
