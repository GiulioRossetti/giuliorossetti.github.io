Research line

Cognitive NetSci

Feature-rich network models of the mental lexicon, language acquisition and emotional profiling of text.

OverviewThree goalsThe mental lexicon as a networkObjectivesPublications

Cognitive network science studies human cognition by modelling the mental lexicon, the store of words and concepts behind thought and communication, as a network.

Traditional psycholinguistics focuses on the properties of individual words, such as valence, arousal or concreteness. Cognitive network science looks at the structure that emerges from their connections. Feature-rich network models bring the two views together, relating word properties and connectivity to language functions and cognitive processes.

Three goals

A feature-rich framework

Network models of the mental lexicon that combine word properties, multiple relation types and time.

Data-driven cognition

Tools and algorithms applied to psycholinguistic experiments, from free associations to early word learning.

Emotional profiling

Methods to extract emotional profiles from user-generated content, and to compare human and LLM associations.

The mental lexicon as a network

How the elements of a feature-rich network model map onto human cognition.

System
Human cognition, mental lexicon, semantic, phonological and syntactic memory.
Nodes
Concepts and words, or their sub-units such as phonemes and morphemes.
Relations
Free associations, synonyms and antonyms, hypernymy, shared features, phonological similarity, syntactic dependencies.
Graph
Words are linked when they share a linguistic similarity, are recalled together, or co-occur in sentences.
Higher-order
Co-occurrences and multi-response association tasks naturally form hyperedges, revealing structures such as knowledge gaps.
Attributes
Length, frequency, polysemy, valence, arousal, dominance and concreteness, related to connectivity through assortative mixing.
Multilayer
Associations, synonyms and phonological similarities as layers, analysed together to reveal patterns in word acquisition.
Dynamic
The lexicon grows as words are learned; acquisition can be modelled as a process, such as a random walk, on an evolving network.

Objectives

  1. 1

    Develop a feature-rich network framework for the study of human cognition, the mental lexicon and language acquisition.

  2. 2

    Pursue data-driven cognitive network science, applying these tools to data from psycholinguistic experiments.

  3. 3

    Define sound methodologies to extract emotional profiles from user-generated content.
